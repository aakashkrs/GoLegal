SHe-Box:

The Government of India has put in place the Protection of Women from Sexual Harassment at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition, and Redressal) Act, 2013 (commonly known as the SH Act) to ensure that women feel safe and secure at their workplace. This Act applies to women in both organized (like offices, companies) and unorganized sectors (like domestic workers, freelancers), whether in public or private spaces. The law aims to provide a proper mechanism for women to report and resolve any cases of sexual harassment they might face at work.

What is Sexual Harassment?

Sexual harassment at the workplace isn’t just about direct physical contact. It also includes:

  • Any promise of benefits or threats to job security related to sexual advances.
  • Creating a hostile or uncomfortable work environment for women.
  • Any form of humiliating treatment that affects a woman’s health or safety.

Introducing SHe-Box

SHe-Box (Sexual Harassment electronic Box) is a digital platform created by the Government of India. It allows women, regardless of where they work or what they do, to easily report sexual harassment at their workplace. Here’s how it works:

  1. Who Can Use It?
    • Any woman facing sexual harassment at work can file a complaint on SHe-Box, whether she works in a government office, a private company, or even from home.
  2. How to Register a Complaint?
    • All you need is a valid email ID. Visit the SHe-Box website, click on "Register Your Complaint," and fill in the required details, such as the type of office (Government/Private) where the harassment occurred.
    • After submitting the complaint, you’ll receive a confirmation email with a link to create a password. This will allow you to track the status of your complaint.

Resources and Support

The Resources Section on SHe-Box is packed with useful information, including:

  • The full text of the SH Act and the rules, available in both Hindi and English.
  • A handbook explaining the SH Act in simple terms, along with a training module to help government officials understand and implement the law. Private companies can also customize this module to fit their policies.
  • Advisories and guidelines from the Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT), which are especially useful for government offices.
  • Videos and other materials that can help raise awareness about sexual harassment at the workplace.

Empanelled Institutes and Resource Persons

To spread awareness and ensure proper training on the SH Act, the Ministry of Women and Child Development (WCD) has partnered with various institutes and organizations. These partners conduct workshops and training programs across the country. Currently, there are 112 such empanelled institutes. They regularly report their activities through SHe-Box.

Additionally, WCD is looking to empanel individuals as Resource Persons who can provide training and support on the SH Act. Details on how to apply will soon be available on the SHe-Box website under the "Join Us" section.

In Summary

SHe-Box is a powerful tool designed to make it easier for women to report and resolve cases of sexual harassment at work. It’s a step towards ensuring that all workplaces in India are safe, respectful, and supportive environments for women.

Steps to Register a Complaint in SHe-Box

  1. Access the SHe-Box Portal:
    • Visit the official SHe-Box website.
  2. Start the Complaint Registration:
    • Click on the "Register Your Complaint" tab on the homepage.
  3. Select Your Employment Category:
    • If you are a government employee, choose whether you work with:
      • Central Government
      • State Government
    • If you are a private sector employee, select Private.
  4. Complete the Registration Form:
    • Fill in all the required details in the registration form, including your employment details and the nature of the complaint.
  5. Submit the Form:
    • After completing the form, press the "Submit" button.
  6. Confirmation Email:
    • A confirmation message will be sent to the email address you provided in the registration form.
  7. Activate Your Account:
    • Open the confirmation email and click on the link "Confirm my email and create my account!"
    • Follow the link to generate a password for your account.
  8. Check Complaint Status:
    • Use the generated email ID as your username and the created password to log in and view the status of your complaint at any time.
  1. What is Sexual Harassment at Workplace?

Sexual harassment at the workplace, under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition, and Redressal) Act, 2013 (SH Act), is any unwelcome act or behavior of a sexual nature, including:

  • Physical contact and advances
  • A demand or request for sexual favors
  • Making sexually colored remarks
  • Showing pornography
  • Any other unwelcome physical, verbal, or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature

This definition is rooted in the landmark Vishaka vs. State of Rajasthan (1997) case, where the Supreme Court laid down the guidelines for preventing sexual harassment at workplaces in India.

  1. How Many Kinds of Sexual Harassment at Workplace Are There?

Sexual harassment at the workplace is categorized into two types:

  • Quid Pro Quo: Submission to or rejection of such conduct is used as the basis for employment decisions.
  • Hostile Work Environment: Conduct creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment.
  1. What is ‘Quid Pro Quo’ Kind of Sexual Harassment at Workplace?

‘Quid Pro Quo’ translates to "this for that." It involves a situation where someone in authority demands sexual favors in exchange for work-related benefits such as promotions, salary hikes, or favorable work assignments. Refusal can lead to negative consequences like demotion or termination. This form of harassment was highlighted in the Vishaka judgment to protect women from coercive practices.

  1. What is ‘Hostile Work Environment’ Kind of Sexual Harassment at Workplace?

A ‘Hostile Work Environment’ occurs when unwelcome conduct of a sexual nature creates an intimidating or offensive work environment, making it difficult for an employee to work. This can include inappropriate jokes, comments, displaying sexually explicit materials, or other actions that hinder an employee's ability to perform their job. This type of harassment was also recognized by the Supreme Court in the Vishaka judgment.

  1. What is Unwelcome Sexual Conduct?

Unwelcome sexual conduct is any behavior of a sexual nature that is not invited, reciprocated, and is considered undesirable or offensive by the recipient. The SH Act emphasizes that the recipient’s perception is key in determining if the conduct was unwelcome.

  1. Who Can Complain About Sexual Harassment at Workplace?

Under the SH Act, any woman who feels subjected to sexual harassment at the workplace can file a complaint. This includes:

  • Employees (regular, temporary, ad-hoc, or daily wage workers)
  • Domestic workers
  • Women working in any capacity (contractual, part-time, or voluntary)
  • Interns, trainees, or apprentices
  1. Does the Act Constituting Sexual Harassment Have to Occur at Work?

No, the SH Act covers incidents beyond the physical workplace, including:

  • Offsite locations: Client meetings, training sessions, and work-related events.
  • Commutes and other work-related places: Restrooms, cafeterias, or transportation provided by the employer.
  1. If I am a Victim of Sexual Harassment, What Should I Do?

If you are a victim of sexual harassment, follow these steps:

  1. Document the Incident: Keep a record of the dates, times, places, and details, along with any witnesses.
  2. Report the Incident: File a complaint with the Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) at your workplace, or with the Local Complaints Committee (LCC) if there is no ICC.
  3. Seek Support: Consult a legal advisor, a trusted colleague, or a women’s organization for advice.
  1. What is Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)?

The Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) is a mandatory body established in every workplace with ten or more employees. The ICC is responsible for:

  • Receiving complaints of sexual harassment
  • Conducting inquiries
  • Recommending actions if the complaint is proved

The ICC must consist of at least four members, with a majority being women, including a senior woman employee as the presiding officer and an external member from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women.

  1. What is Local Complaints Committee (LCC)?

The Local Complaints Committee (LCC) is set up at the district level by the District Officer for workplaces with fewer than ten employees or where there is no ICC. It also handles cases of domestic workers. The LCC has similar powers and functions as the ICC, ensuring that women in smaller organizations or unorganized sectors have access to justice.

  1. How to Locate ICC Within Your Organisation?

To locate the ICC within your organization:

  • Check the Employee Handbook: ICC details may be listed in the employee handbook or intranet.
  • Contact HR: Reach out to the Human Resources department for information.
  • Look for Notices: Some organizations display ICC details on notice boards or official websites.
  1. How to Locate LCC in a Particular District?

To locate the LCC in a district:

  • Contact the District Officer: Usually the District Magistrate or Deputy Commissioner.
  • Visit the District Office: Information on the LCC can be found at district-level offices or local government websites.
  • Check Government Portals: Some states list LCC details on their women’s welfare department’s website or other related portals.
