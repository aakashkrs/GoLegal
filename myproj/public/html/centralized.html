ALL ABOUT CPGRAM PORTAL

The Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS) is a vital initiative by the Government of India to empower citizens in addressing grievances and monitoring their resolution efficiently.

KEY POINTS ABOUT THE CENTRALISED PUBLIC GRIEVANCE REDRESS AND MONITORING SYSTEM (CPGRAMS):

  • It is an online platform available 24x7 for citizens to lodge grievances related to service delivery to public authorities.

  • It is a single portal connected to all Ministries/Departments of the Government of India and States.

  • Every Ministry and State has role-based access to the system.

  • CPGRAMS provides an appeal facility to citizens if they are not satisfied with the resolution by the Grievance Officer.

  • After closure of the grievance, if the complainant is not satisfied with the resolution, they can provide feedback.

  • If the feedback rating is 'Poor', the option to file an appeal is enabled.

  • Citizens can track the status of their appeals using the grievance registration number.

ISSUES WHICH ARE NOT TAKEN UP FOR REDRESS

  • RTI

  • Court Related/ Sub-judice matter

  • Religious matters

  • Grievances of Government Employees concerning their service matters including disciplinary proceedings etc.

FUNCTIONAL OF CPGRAM:

  • The CPGRAM Grievance and PG Portal offer a simplified process for filing complaints and checking their status, ensuring transparency and accountability in government services.

  • Users can select the specific government department related to their complaint, making it easier for the authorities to address the issue promptly.

  • The portal allows users to provide detailed descriptions of their grievances, ensuring that the concerned authorities have all the necessary information to resolve the issue effectively.

  • Attachments such as PDF files can be included to support the complaint, providing additional evidence or relevant documents.

  • The registration number received after successful submission of the complaint serves as a reference for future communication and tracking of the complaint.

  • The CPGRAM Grievance and PG Portal contribute to citizen empowerment by enabling them to voice their concerns and hold the government accountable for addressing their grievances.

  • The user-friendly interface and step-by-step guidance provided on the portal make it accessible to individuals with varying levels of technological proficiency.

STEPS TO FOLLOW FOR LODGING GRIEVANCE

  • Upon logging in, citizens navigate to the "Lodge Grievance" section to file a new complaint.

  • Here, citizens select the relevant ministry/department/organization pertaining to their grievance from the dropdown list.

  • Citizens must provide detailed information about their grievance, including a brief summary, description of the issue, and relevant background information to aid understanding and resolution.

  • Supporting documents, such as correspondence copies or receipts, can be uploaded to strengthen the case.

  • Before submission, citizens must review all provided details for accuracy and completeness to ensure effective processing of the complaint.
  • Upon successful submission, citizens receive a unique registration number (grievance number) for tracking the progress and status of their complaint.

IF THE ISSUE IS STILL PENDING, AND NO STATUS BEEN PROVIDED OR UPDATED, WITHIN THE TIME FRAME, THEN HOW TO ASK FOR A STATUS REPORT ON A FILED COMPLAINT.

Requesting a status report:

  • If your complaint has not been addressed within the specified timeframe, you can request a status report from the concerned department.

  • The CPGRAM portal may have a dedicated section or form for requesting status reports on filed complaints.

  • Alternatively, you can send a written request (e-mail or physical letter) to the concerned department or the nodal redressal officer, clearly mentioning your grievance registration number and requesting an update on the status.

  • In your request, you should provide your contact details (name, address, email, phone number) and any relevant details about your complaint to facilitate a prompt response.

FILING AN RTI FOR SEEKING INFORMATION:

  • If you are not satisfied with the response or lack of information regarding the status of your complaint, you can file a request under the Right to Information (RTI) Act.

  • The CPGRAM portal should provide guidance on how to file an RTI request, including the process, fees (if applicable), and the necessary contact details for the designated RTI officer of the concerned department.

  • Typically, you need to submit a written application (online or physical) to the Public Information Officer (PIO) of the concerned department, clearly stating the information you are seeking and providing your contact details.

  • You may need to pay the prescribed fee (if applicable) for filing the RTI request.

  • The RTI Act mandates that the requested information should be provided within 30 days of receiving the application, subject to certain exceptions and exemptions.

IF RTI IS NOT RESPONDED TO WITHIN 30 DAYS:

  • If your RTI request is not responded to within 30 days of receiving the application, you have the right to file a first appeal with the designated First Appellate Authority.

  • The CPGRAM portal or the concerned department's website should provide the contact information (name, designation, postal address, phone number, and email ID) of the First Appellate Authority.

  • To file the first appeal, you need to submit a written application to the First Appellate Authority, stating the reasons for the appeal and providing relevant details, such as the RTI application number, date of filing, and the concerned department.

  • You should also include copies of the original RTI application and any supporting documents or evidence.

FILING THE FIRST APPEAL

  • The process for filing the first appeal typically involves submitting a written application (online or physical) to the designated First Appellate Authority within the prescribed timeframe (usually 30 days from the date of expected response or from the last date of the period specified for responding to the RTI request).

  • In your appeal, you should clearly state the grounds for appeal, such as the non-receipt of information or dissatisfaction with the response provided by the Public Information Officer (PIO).

  • Provide relevant details, such as the RTI application number, date of filing, and the concerned department.

  • Include copies of the original RTI application, any correspondence with the PIO, and any supporting documents or evidence.

  • The First Appellate Authority is expected to dispose of the appeal within the time period specified in the RTI Act (usually within 30 days of receiving the appeal).

FILING THE SECOND APPEAL:

  • If you are not satisfied with the outcome of the first appeal, you can file a second appeal with the appropriate authority, which is typically the Central Information Commission (CIC) or the State Information Commission (SIC), depending on whether the concerned department is under the central government or state government.

  • The CPGRAM portal or the concerned department's website should provide the contact information (name, designation, postal address, phone number, and email ID) of the CIC or SIC, as well as the procedures for filing a second appeal.

  • The process for filing the second appeal is similar to the first appeal and involves submitting a written application to the CIC or SIC within the prescribed timeframe (usually 90 days from the date of the decision of the First Appellate Authority).

  • In your appeal, you should clearly state the grounds for appeal, provide relevant details (such as the RTI application number, first appeal details, and the concerned department), and include copies of all relevant documents and correspondence.

  • The CIC or SIC will conduct a hearing and issue a decision on your appeal, which is binding on the concerned department.

APPROACHING THE COURT OF LAW:

  • As a last resort, citizens unsatisfied with the outcome of the second appeal have the option to approach the relevant High Court or Supreme Court of India for relief. Consulting a legal professional is advisable to ensure adherence to correct legal procedures, requirements, and jurisdictions for filing a case in court.

  • Throughout the grievance redressal process, the CPGRAM portal and respective ministry/department websites offer comprehensive information, guidelines, and resources to assist citizens. It is imperative for citizens to review and adhere to the procedures outlined on these platforms or seek guidance from concerned authorities for a smooth and effective resolution of complaints.
